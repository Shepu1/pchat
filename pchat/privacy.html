1. 🔎 The short version

  • There is no Pchat account and no Pchat message server. The app is a set of static files. Nothing is sent to the people who wrote it.
  • A saved room's history is written to a Google Sheet in the room owner's Google Drive. The owner controls that file, not us.
  • A quick room has no backend at all. Its messages live in the browsers of the people in the room, plus the public relay described below.
  • Messages also travel through public relay servers so they arrive across networks. In a room with a passphrase, those servers see ciphertext instead of the message text.
  • No analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixels, no selling data. The app sets no cookies of its own.

The rest of this page explains each of those points, including the parts that are less comfortable.

2. 🏠 Who is responsible for your data

Pchat is self-hosted software: one folder of HTML, CSS and JavaScript that anyone can put on any web host. There is no company behind this particular copy that receives your messages.

If someone sent you a room link, that person is your first point of contact. They chose where the app is hosted and they usually own the room. The room owner is the one who controls the saved history, because it is stored in their own Google Drive.

If you are running this copy of the app yourself, then you are the operator: you choose the host, and the privacy commitments on this page describe what the software does — not a service agreement from a third party.

3. 🚫 What this app does not do

  • No sign-up. You can use a quick room without a name, an email or a password.
  • No central message database. There is no server that stores your conversations.
  • No advertising or analytics. There is no Google Analytics, no Meta pixel, no session recording, no fingerprinting script and no A/B testing service.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data. There is no business model that needs it.
  • No reading of your messages for ads or AI training. The one exception is when you explicitly ask the Shepu-AI bot something — see section 9.
  • No app cookies. The app never calls document.cookie. It stores state in localStorage, sessionStorage and IndexedDB instead (section 4).

5. 🗂️ What is saved in a room

There are two kinds of room, and they store data very differently.

Quick rooms have no backend. Messages travel directly between members (and over the relay) and each member's browser keeps its own copy. The app itself stores no copy on a server; what was relayed remains only as long as the relay's own retention.

Saved rooms use a Google Sheet in the owner's Drive. The owner's device writes each message there as a row:

Sheet tabColumns
messagesmessage ID, timestamp, sender ID, sender name, type, body, reply-to ID, edited flag, deleted flag
membersmember ID, display name, joined time, last seen, online status, role, removed flag, active flag, session token
room_configthe room's settings: name, theme, wallpapers, lock state and similar

The spreadsheet also gets three empty helper tabs — attachments, moderation and sync_state — created with headers when the room is made. This version of the app does not write rows to them.

The message body is whatever was sent: plain text, an attachment encoded as a data URL, or ciphertext starting with enc: when a passphrase is in use. Attachments therefore count against the owner's Google Drive storage.

Guests never receive the owner's Google credentials or direct edit access to the Sheet; their messages reach it through the room's transport. The owner, however, can open the Sheet at any time: a saved room without a passphrase is private, not secret, from the owner.

Deleting a room in the app removes it from your list; to remove the history permanently, the owner also deletes the Sheet from Google Drive.

6. 📡 The public relay

For instant delivery across networks, the app publishes each event to several independent public ntfy servers. The defaults are ntfy.sh, ntfy.envs.net, ntfy.adminforge.de and ntfy.mzte.de. You can change this list in Settings → Relay.

What those servers receive: a topic name, plus a JSON envelope containing the message ID, sender ID and display name, timestamp, message type, reply-to ID and the message body.

Which topic: a private name derived from the room's random secret. For compatibility with older invite links, a normal room also publishes to a legacy topic named after the room ID (shepu_pchat_<roomId>). A password-locked room uses the private topic only. Anyone who knows a room's ID can subscribe to its legacy topic, which is why the private topic exists and why a passphrase matters.

What the body is: ciphertext (enc:…) in a room with a passphrase. In a room without one, it is plain text — readable by whoever operates the relay, and by anyone who obtains the topic. The private topic is not guessable without the invite link, but it is not a secret the relay keeps for you.

Relay operators may log connection IP addresses and may retain messages for a period (typically hours) under their own policies. We do not control those servers and cannot delete data from them. Treat them as post offices, not vaults.

This is why the app offers encryption, and why the honest answer is: use a passphrase for anything you would not want a stranger to read.

7. 🔐 Encryption, exactly

When a room has a passphrase, messages are encrypted on your device, before they leave it:

  • AES-256-GCM with a fresh random 12-byte IV for every message.
  • The key is derived from the passphrase with PBKDF2-SHA-256, 100,000 iterations.
  • Ciphertext is prefixed with enc: so the app knows to decrypt it.
  • The key stays in this tab's memory (and, for a password-locked room, in session storage). It is never sent to Google, to the relay, or to any other server.

The invite link carries a random room secret in the URL fragment (the part after #). Browsers do not send that fragment to web servers, and it is what makes the relay topic unguessable. That automatic key lets a guest read earlier history, but it does not encrypt new messages — only a passphrase does.

What encryption protects: the content of messages and attachments while they sit in the Sheet or pass through the relay.

What it does not protect: who talked to whom, when, how often, and message sizes. It also cannot protect a room without a passphrase, a screenshot, a compromised device, or someone who has the passphrase. And a forgotten passphrase cannot be recovered — the key is not stored anywhere it could be reset from.

8. 🔑 Google sign-in and Drive

Google sign-in is optional. It is only needed to create or back up a saved room, because that room uses your own Drive.

When you sign in, the app receives your name, email address, profile picture and Google account ID from Google, plus an access token. The token is stored in localStorage on this device and expires on its own. You can revoke access at any time from your Google account permissions.

The scopes requested are openid, email and profile. Creating a saved room additionally asks for Drive file access, Sheets access, and Apps Script project/deployment access — that is what lets the app create the spreadsheet and the room endpoint in your account.

The app does not send your Google profile or token anywhere else. Google's own handling of your account is covered by Google's privacy policy.

9. 🤖 The Shepu-AI bot

The bot in the chat is opt-in: it only wakes up when someone writes @shepu-ai. Most answers come from a reference database bundled with the app, with no network request at all.

When the bundled data does not cover a question, the bot asks an external text service (Pollinations, at text.pollinations.ai) to compose an answer. That request contains your question, your display name, and the last six messages of your conversation with the bot — not the room's general chat. The request asks the service to treat it as private and sends no credentials and no referrer.

Image requests work differently: the prompt becomes part of a link to image.pollinations.ai, and every member's browser loads that link. The prompt is therefore visible to that service and to anyone who can see the message.

The app does not write AI training data to Google Sheets, and the bot does not read your room history to learn from it. Still: do not give the bot passwords, private documents or anything you would not put in a search engine.

10. 📞 Calls

Audio and video calls use WebRTC. The media normally travels directly between the people on the call, encrypted in transit with DTLS-SRTP, which WebRTC requires.

To establish that connection the app uses Google's public STUN servers to discover network addresses, and PeerJS's public TURN servers when a direct path is impossible. On a TURN relay, the media passes through that server, still encrypted. As with any call, the other participants can see your IP address, and the infrastructure providers can see that a connection exists.

The app does not record calls and does not store audio or video anywhere.

11. 🔔 Notifications

Notifications are generated locally by a service worker. They are not sent through a push service, and no message content goes to a notification provider.

A notification shows the sender's name and a short preview of the message. That preview can appear on your lock screen or in your notification history, where other people or your operating system may see it. If that matters to you, turn notifications off in Settings → Notifications, or in your browser's site settings.

The service worker deliberately caches nothing: it exists only to display notifications and to focus the right tab when one is tapped.

12. 🌐 Other requests the page makes

  • Google Fonts. The stylesheet loads three font families from fonts.googleapis.com, so Google sees your IP address and browser when the page loads. No account information is sent with that request.
  • Google sign-in. The Google Identity script is loaded from accounts.google.com only when sign-in is used or needed.
  • QR codes are generated on your device by a bundled library. If that library were unavailable, the fallback strips the secret fragment from the link before sending it to a QR image service.
  • No other third-party scripts, fonts, pixels or widgets are loaded. Everything else — the icons, the emoji set, the chat engine — ships with the app.

14. ✅ Your choices

  • Leave a room at any time; your local copy can be cleared in Settings → Privacy.
  • Rotate the room link if it has been shared too widely. Old links stop working for new joins.
  • Set a passphrase before saying anything sensitive. Without one, the owner and the relay can read the content.
  • Turn notifications off so previews do not appear on your screen.
  • Avoid the bot if you do not want a question sent to an external AI service. Ordinary chat does not involve it.
  • Use Google sign-in only when you need a saved room. A quick room needs no account at all.

15. 🧒 Children

This app is not directed at children under 13 (or the minimum age required in your country). It has no age verification, because it has no accounts and collects no personal data itself. A parent or guardian who runs a room should decide whether it is appropriate for the people they invite.

16. 📝 Changes to this policy

This page ships with the app, so it changes when the app changes. The date at the top is the last time the text was updated. If a change affects what leaves your device, it will be reflected in the sections above — there is no separate mailing list to notify you through.

17. ✉️ Contact

For questions about a specific room, contact the person who sent you the link — they own the room and its data. For questions about this copy of the app, contact whoever hosts it. There is no central support desk, because there is no central service.
